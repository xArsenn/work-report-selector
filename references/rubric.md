# Finance-oriented daily report rubric

Use this rubric as a prediction model for likely selection, not as confirmed company policy unless the user explicitly confirms it.

## Score dimensions (100 points)

### 1. Result and closure — 30

- 26–30: Major deliverable completed, launched, accepted, collected, settled, or verified; final state is unambiguous.
- 19–25: Substantial milestone completed, but part of the outcome remains pending.
- 10–18: Useful progress with identifiable output, but mostly intermediate work.
- 0–9: Activities, meetings, or plans without a clear delivered result.

### 2. Evidence and measurability — 25

- 21–25: Multiple auditable metrics, amounts, before/after comparisons, acceptance evidence, or named deliverables.
- 15–20: At least one meaningful quantity or clearly checkable outcome.
- 8–14: Some specifics, but impact or completion is hard to verify.
- 0–7: Vague claims such as “积极推进”“处理很多”“效果很好”.

Do not reward decorative numbers such as dates, list numbering, or counts that do not establish output or impact.

Cap this dimension at 15 when the report supplies high volume but no quality, acceptance, conversion, outcome, or other evidence that the volume was useful.

### 3. Business value — 20

- 17–20: Direct material effect on revenue, cash, cost, efficiency, acquisition, delivery, data accuracy, compliance, or risk.
- 12–16: Clear operational value with plausible downstream business impact.
- 6–11: Team-local usefulness, weakly connected to an organizational objective.
- 0–5: Business relevance is unclear.

Evaluate value relative to the employee's role. Do not compare content volume directly with engineering latency, legal risk, or settlement amounts.

Risk prevention and avoided loss can be high business value even when no revenue is created. Do not invent a monetary value for avoided risk.

### 4. Difficulty and problem closure — 15

- 13–15: Important obstacle resolved with cause, action, and verification; coordination or technical complexity is clear.
- 9–12: Meaningful problem solved, but cause or verification is incomplete.
- 4–8: Routine execution or a problem with only a partial response.
- 0–3: No meaningful challenge or only an unresolved complaint.

### 5. Review and follow-through — 10

- 9–10: Explicit prior target comparison plus a concrete next commitment, owner, or deadline.
- 6–8: Credible review or specific next action.
- 3–5: Generic plan or reflection.
- 0–2: No follow-through signal.

## Deductions

Apply conservatively and never double-penalize the same weakness:

- −3 to −8: Inflated activity list with no result state.
- −2 to −6: Important claim lacks enough context to interpret the number.
- −3 to −10: Contradictory, misleading, or unsubstantiated completion claim.
- −2 to −5: Problem is described but neither owned nor converted into a corrective action.
- −3 to −8: Volume is presented as value while quality, acceptance, conversion, or downstream use is contradicted or conspicuously absent.
- −3 to −10: Wording upgrades a pending state into a completed state or implies causality unsupported by the source.

Do not deduct merely for short length, imperfect formatting, missing a next-day plan, or missing a target when the report supplies a strong variance explanation and corrective action.

## Interpretation

- 85–100: Strong winner
- 75–84: Likely shortlist / winner depending on quota
- 65–74: Solid report, but not automatically outstanding
- 50–64: Mostly routine or under-evidenced
- Below 50: Unlikely to be selected

Use these bands as defaults. When scoring a batch, relative ranking and the user's quota matter more than a fixed cutoff.

For single-report revisions, score only facts present in the copy-ready version. Placeholders and suggested evidence receive zero points until confirmed.

## Calibration with labeled examples

When both winners and non-winners are supplied:

1. Score all examples using the current rubric before changing weights.
2. Identify recurring features that separate winners from non-winners within similar job families.
3. Change a weight only when the separating pattern recurs across several samples; do not overfit one example.
4. Record the revised weight, supporting evidence, and confidence level in the response.
5. Preserve the five-dimension total of 100 unless the user explicitly requests another system.
