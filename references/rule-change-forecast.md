# Leadership rule-change forecast

Use this reference when the user asks what leadership may change or when improving a daily or weekly report. The forecast is a robustness aid, not a claim about unpublished policy.

## Forecast inputs

Use, in descending order of reliability:

1. Confirmed leadership announcements or actual revised rules.
2. Time-ordered labeled winners and non-winners after a known change.
3. Repeated patterns across recent confirmed winners.
4. Obvious scoring vulnerabilities that employees could game.
5. The decision-maker's stated function or current company priorities, when supplied by the user.

Do not infer private intent from personality, job title alone, or a single unusual winner.

## Forecast categories

Consider only categories supported by available evidence:

- **Weight shift:** more or less emphasis on results, evidence, business value, risk, systemization, or continuity.
- **New gate:** verification, minimum completion state, strategic alignment, or mandatory fields.
- **Anti-gaming:** less credit for raw volume, verbose lists, repeated metrics, formulaic AI language, or unsupported value claims.
- **Role normalization:** scoring relative to role, department, controllability, or work type.
- **Selection mechanics:** quota, department balance, repeat-winner handling, or comparable peer groups.
- **Longitudinal consistency:** checking yesterday/last week commitments against current delivery.

## Confidence

Use qualitative confidence only:

- **High:** supported by several recent samples, a confirmed pain point, or a clear exploitable weakness plus decision-maker incentives.
- **Medium:** plausible and supported indirectly, but alternative explanations remain.
- **Low:** speculative, sample-dependent, or unsupported by comparison data.

Return at most three directions by default. Exclude low-confidence directions unless the user explicitly asks for a broad scenario scan.

For each direction state:

1. What may change.
2. Why the evidence points there.
3. How it affects this report.
4. The smallest truthful edit or missing fact that makes the report robust.

## Robustness gate

An edit is robust when it:

- Improves or preserves the current score.
- Also helps under at least one high-confidence forecast.
- Does not rely on an unverifiable metric or invented causal claim.
- Keeps completion states precise.
- Does not make the report materially longer without adding decision value.

Prefer invariant strengths: real completed outcomes, audit-ready evidence, business relevance, quality or verification, honest pending states, and a concrete next node. These survive most reasonable rule changes.

## Updating after leadership changes

When the actual modification becomes available:

1. Record it as confirmed policy, replacing the corresponding forecast.
2. Compare predicted versus actual direction.
3. Update daily and weekly rubrics only where the confirmed change applies.
4. Preserve older labeled observations as historical context; do not silently rewrite them as if they used the new rule.
5. Report which forecast signals were correct, incorrect, or still unresolved.
